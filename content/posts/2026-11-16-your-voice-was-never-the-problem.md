Most people who tell me they are not confident speakers are measuring themselves against someone else. They have a picture in their head of what a good speaker sounds like, and it usually comes with a particular accent, a particular pace and a particular kind of certainty. Then they listen to themselves and decide they fall short.

I understand why. We are surrounded by examples of how a voice is supposed to sound, and very few of them sound like us. Comparison does quiet work. It rarely tells you to stop speaking altogether, and it often suggests that you wait until you sound more like somebody else.

What I have learnt, after working with people in very different rooms around the world, is that the voice itself is rarely what needs fixing. The accent is fine. The pace can be worked on. What usually needs attention is the belief that your natural way of speaking is a rough draft of a better person's.

So here is where I would start. Record yourself talking for one minute about something you care about, and listen back once, as if you were listening to a friend. Notice what is already working before you look for what to change. That small habit teaches you to hear your own voice as yours.

You were made on purpose, and that includes the way you sound. Confidence grows from using your voice often enough that it stops feeling like a performance.
