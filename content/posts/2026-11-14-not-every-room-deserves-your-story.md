We are often told to tell our story, as though sharing more is always the brave choice. I believe in stories, and I have built my working life around helping people tell theirs. I also believe that a story has consequences once it leaves you.

Every story lands in a room, and rooms are different. Some are ready to listen. Some have already decided who is worth listening to before anyone speaks, on the basis of accent, age, seniority or background. A story that would be received with care in one place can be misread, or used, in another.

Choosing where to share is part of the craft. Before I tell a personal story, I ask what I want the listener to understand, whether this audience has earned that level of trust, and what it might cost me to say it here. Sometimes the answer is to tell a smaller version. Sometimes it is to wait for a different room.

You can do all of this and still speak up often. Your story is valuable, and where it goes is your decision. When you do find the right room, you will feel the difference, and so will the people listening.
