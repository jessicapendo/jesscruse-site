When people ask me for help with their voice, they often mean their words. They want a better opening line, a tighter pitch or a stronger answer to a difficult question. Those things matter, and I enjoy working on them.

Over time I have come to see voice as something wider. It includes what we say and how we say it, and it also includes what is happening inside us while we speak. Two people can deliver the same sentence, and one of them will be enjoying the moment while the other is waiting for it to end.

That inner experience shapes everything the listener receives. Breath, pace, eye contact and warmth all follow from how safe and how present we feel. This is why rehearsing a script alone only takes you so far. The script improves, and the feeling in your chest stays the same.

A more useful practice is to work on both together. Before you speak, take one slow breath and decide who you are speaking for. While you speak, let yourself pause. Afterwards, ask yourself how it felt as well as how it went.

When we find our voice, nurture it and share it, our world changes, and other people's can too. That begins with making the experience of speaking one you would be willing to repeat.
